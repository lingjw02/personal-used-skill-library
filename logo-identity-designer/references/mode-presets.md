# Creative mode presets

Each preset below is a **delta** on top of the core workflow in SKILL.md -- it changes emphasis, question priorities, and defaults. None of them remove a required deliverable from the production gate. Load only the section(s) relevant to the brief in front of you.

All example brands in this file are fictional demonstration briefs invented for illustration. Their names have not been trademark-cleared and should not be presented to a user as available names.

---

## Minimal

**Best for:** rapid ideation, non-designers, early-stage founders, or a designer who already knows exactly what they want and just needs it executed fast.

**What changes:**
- Brief depth: low. Ask for brand name, industry, 3 values, audience, visual direction, colors, and what to avoid -- skip the rest unless offered.
- Exploration: 3 concepts, lighter rationale per concept than Detailed mode.
- Skip extended competitive audits and deep strategic framing; move quickly from brief to concepts.
- Still require: monochrome version, icon-only version, horizontal + vertical lockups, small-size legibility check, SVG/PNG/PDF + color values + font suggestions. Speed doesn't waive production requirements.

**Worked example (brief → concept seed):**
"Arclume," an architectural-lighting brand. Values: precision, warmth, quiet confidence. Audience: architects and interior designers. Direction: minimal, architectural, geometric. Colors: charcoal, warm ivory, optional muted amber. Icon idea: an abstract relationship between an arc and a controlled beam of light. Avoid: lightbulb clip art, sunburst rays, generic house-roof shapes, 3D effects. One resulting concept: turn the diagonal stem of an "A" into a narrow light beam crossing a single arc, tested first as a plain black silhouette before any color or mockup is added.

## Detailed (default)

**Best for:** the general-purpose production workflow -- most real branding projects, freelance and agency work, and any logo meant to survive past a quick prototype. Use this as the base for `auto` mode unless another preset's signal is clearly stronger.

**What changes:**
- Full brief: business context, positioning, brand story, desired/undesired perception, references (as attributes), competitors, full application list, aspect ratios, formats, budget/timeline, trademark-screening level.
- Exploration: exactly 3 territories -- one conservative, one balanced, one more distinctive/experimental -- each with symbolism, form language, typography, color, benefits, and risk spelled out.
- Full evaluation pass on all 3 (distinctiveness, legibility, small-size, monochrome, digital use, print use) before recommending one.
- Full deliverable set per `references/production-delivery.md`, including a mini style guide with clear-space and minimum-size rules.

**Worked example (brief → concept seed):**
"Harborleaf," a premium sustainable home-goods company. Positioning: premium but approachable, not luxury-fashion and not "eco startup." Must not feel rustic-craft-fair, clinical, or activist. Colors: deep forest green, warm clay, off-white (no fluorescent green). References: quiet editorial packaging, Scandinavian restraint -- translated into attributes like generous negative space and a restrained palette, not copied directly. Icon direction: harbor/shelter plus organic growth, explicitly avoiding a literal house-roof-and-leaf icon. The balanced concept route might build a shelter-like negative space between two abstract leaf forms paired with a custom-spaced wordmark; the distinctive route might abstract shoreline-contour geometry instead of botanical imagery.

## Creative-exploratory

**Best for:** entertainment, fashion, music, culture, creative studios, youth brands, or any brief where distinctiveness matters more than category familiarity.

**What changes:**
- Explore **5** strongly differentiated territories before narrowing to 3, not 3 from the start. At least one territory must be purely typographic, one symbolic, one abstract, and at least one should feel conceptually surprising or radically simplified.
- References are translated into qualities like energy, rhythm, asymmetry, texture, contrast, or era -- never copied.
- After narrowing to the strongest 3, explicitly test each in black-and-white, at 32-64px icon scale, as a horizontal wordmark, as a social avatar, and against one realistic real-world application.
- Guardrail: novelty must never override legibility of the brand name -- flag it if a direction gets there.

**Worked example (brief → concept seed):**
"Mothwave," an independent music-discovery platform for underground/experimental/electronic listeners. Feel: mysterious, intelligent, kinetic, cult-like but welcoming. Avoid generic headphones, equalizer bars, music notes, or Spotify-style circles. Imagery pool: moth navigation, interference patterns, night signals, attraction to light. One route reduces a moth wing into four frequency bands; another constructs the letter "M" from opposing waveform phases; an experimental wordmark alters selected letter terminals to suggest signal interference without harming readability.

## Corporate

**Best for:** finance, professional services, B2B, insurance, consulting, institutional organizations, regulated industries -- anywhere trust and long-term stability dominate the brief.

**What changes:**
- Explicitly explore 3 routes: (A) wordmark-led, (B) monogram or abstract mark, (C) symbol + wordmark.
- Explicitly avoid: fashionable effects with a short visual shelf life, excessive ornament, generic stock-corporate symbols (shields, handshakes, checkmarks, courthouse columns, generic growth arrows), and any complexity that disappears when shrunk into a document header.
- Production behavior is non-negotiable here: excellent black-and-white reproduction, legibility in document headers/decks, a strong horizontal lockup, conservative small-size behavior, and no dependency on gradients.
- Explicitly explain why the recommended direction avoids visual confusion with the named competitors -- this is expected output, not optional.

**Worked example (brief → concept seed):**
"Crestwell Advisory," a B2B risk-and-compliance consultancy for CFOs, legal teams, and boards. Positioning: senior expertise without the coldness of a giant consultancy. Avoid shields, checkmarks, courthouse columns, handshakes, swooshes, generic growth arrows. Colors: deep navy, slate, white, no gradient dependency. Preferred outcome: a typographically led "CRESTWELL" wordmark with a small abstract structural emblem, rather than an over-symbolized "security" logo -- with a monogram route explored only if it turns out genuinely distinctive rather than generic.

## Vintage

**Best for:** hospitality, food and beverage, craft businesses, heritage products, barbers, motorcycles, workshops, local retail, or any brand that needs deliberate historical character.

**What changes:**
- Ask for the *specific* historical/visual influence (an era, a movement, a regional sign-painting tradition) rather than "make it vintage" -- a generic "old-timey" badge is a failure mode to actively avoid.
- Explore 3 routes: heritage wordmark, emblem/badge, and a simplified historical symbol.
- Texture/distressing may appear in packaging or presentation mockups, but the underlying master logo must also exist as clean vector shapes that work without any distressing.
- Explicitly avoid: generic circular coffee-shop-style seals, fake founding dates, illegible decorative scripts, and over-detailed engraving that disappears at small sizes.
- Test as: a one-color stamp, on packaging/a label, on signage, as a small digital avatar, and as the clean non-distressed master.

**Worked example (brief → concept seed):**
"Copper Finch Coffee Roasters," an independent urban roaster influenced by early-to-mid-20th-century packaging and sign painting (not a historical replica). Colors: copper/rust, cream, charcoal. Typography: sturdy serif or slab serif with a small amount of custom hand lettering. Avoid mustaches, crossed tools, fake founding dates, overly detailed engraved birds. Concept: a compact finch silhouette on a custom slab-serif "Copper Finch" wordmark, with a secondary circular stamp version *derived from* -- not substituted for -- the clean master identity.

## Tech / startup

**Best for:** SaaS, AI, developer tools, cloud services, fintech, software, data infrastructure, robotics, or any digital product that needs a strong app-icon/avatar symbol.

**What changes:**
- Explore **4** directions: (1) product-mechanism metaphor, (2) abstract geometric system, (3) distinctive wordmark, (4) compact app-icon-first symbol.
- Explicitly avoid unless strategically justified: generic AI sparkle, random neural-network nodes, a generic "circuit brain," a default hexagon, a meaningless infinity loop, a generic upward arrow, robots, rockets, lightning bolts.
- If a gradient is used anywhere, also produce a fully functional flat one-color variant -- gradients cannot be the only version.
- Rank concepts explicitly on: distinctiveness, product relevance, small-size/favicon recognition, technical credibility, and likelihood of reading as a generic "AI startup" logo (lower is better).
- Test at app/favicon scale, in a website header, in monochrome, in dark mode, and inside a product UI context -- not just on a plain background.

**Worked example (brief → concept seed):**
"Metrivox," a workflow-automation SaaS that routes work across tools via configurable logic, aimed at ops teams without requiring code. Feel: technical but approachable, fast but not aggressive. Colors: deep cobalt or near-black base, optional cyan accent, must work in monochrome. Avoid brain/robot-head icons, sparkles, generic network spheres, circuit traces, an unjustified hexagon. One route depicts a single input path splitting into three organized output modules; another builds the "M" from connected workflow segments; the wordmark route customizes the central letterforms rather than bolting on a generic technology glyph.
