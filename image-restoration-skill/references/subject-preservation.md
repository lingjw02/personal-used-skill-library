# Subject Preservation Details

These are the region-specific rules for restoring a subject without changing who or what they are.

## Face restoration

Prioritize identity consistency above all else. Recover where possible: eye definition, eyelashes, eyebrows, nose contour, mouth contour, hairline, facial boundaries, natural skin or illustration texture.

Never: change facial proportions, beautify unnecessarily, change ethnicity, change age, change expression, change eye shape, change makeup, change hairstyle, or replace the face with a generic "attractive" face. Face enhancement restores the existing identity — it is not a face swap or an upgrade.

## Hair restoration

Improve: hair silhouette, strand separation, major locks, highlights, shadow boundaries, fine edge strands.

Don't invent excessive individual strand detail if the original style uses simplified anime or painted hair — match the rendering language of the source. A flat-shaded anime hair block should stay a flat-shaded hair block, just cleaner, not suddenly photorealistic.

## Clothing and accessory restoration

Recover: seams, fabric folds, embroidery, patterns, jewelry, buttons, buckles, decorative trim, armor edges, printed graphics, costume silhouettes.

Don't invent new patterns or decorations unless required to repair a visibly missing/damaged section, and even then infer from the visible portion of the same pattern. Maintain left/right symmetry where the original design is symmetric.

## Texture restoration

Restore textures according to material type: skin, hair, fur, fabric, leather, metal, glass, stone, wood, plastic, water, foliage.

Keep textures consistent with the original style and scale — a stylized illustration's "metal" texture shouldn't suddenly render like a physically-based 3D metal shader. Avoid a generic synthetic "AI texture" look or excessive micro-detail that wasn't implied by the source.
